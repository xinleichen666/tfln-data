Resonant acousto-optic frequency shifting in a thin-film lithium niobate photonic
molecule: design, tolerances, and a measurement plan
Xinlei Chen1 and Yuanhua Li1
1Shanghai University of Electric Power, Shanghai, China
(Dated: October 14, 2026)
We present a design and measurement plan for a thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) frequency
shifter in which a suspended interdigital-transducer (IDT) acoustic resonator drives one ring of a
dual-ring photonic molecule in a forward, single-bus geometry at a fixed shift of 3.33 GHz, intended
for fabrication. All device numbers below are simulation predictions. Against an electro-optic (EO)
drive of the same optics, the Shao-calibrated IDT needs ≈6.7 µW while a matched EO feed needs 1.2–
13 µW: we do not claim an acousto-optic (AO) power advantage; the AO RF bandwidth is ∼1 MHz.
Versus the measured traveling-wave AO shifter of Shao et al. (3.5% on-chip absolute efficiency at
1 W, 70 MHz bandwidth), we compare absolute efficiency only and state in the same sentence that
our result is simulated at ∼1 MHz bandwidth. With a self-labeled thermo-optic Mach–Zehnder
(MZI) ring–ring coupler that tunes the supermode splitting 2µ (heater on; Pπ = 44.4 mW as a
Maeder geometric assumption), a 1000-sample Monte Carlo under conservative extra loss (κi×2) and
10% IDT efficiency predicts a median ηshift = 96.4% (10th–90th percentile 91.7–98.3%); the heater-
off fixed-gap case (median 94.5%; 74.2% of samples at ηshift ≥90%) is retained only as a comparison.
Insertion loss is reported in two columns: Shao 2019 suspended-racetrack Qi as measured (heater-on
median 2.48 dB) and assumed extra κi × 2 (median 4.72 dB). A partial-coverage factor ξ multiplies
the extrapolated δω/2π = 21.3 GHz/V (ξ = 1); at ηIDT = 10% and Qm = 1570, source power is
478/172/43/11 µW for ξ = 0.3/0.5/1/2. For stage-1 spectral tests, unfiltered 1 −V ≈1 −ηshift
(ideal ≈1.7 × 10−2). Thermal phonons give a drive jitter nth/Ncoh ≈1.07 × 10−8 and an upper
bound nthC0 ≈8.0×10−6 for spontaneous scattering on the same acoustic mode (g0/2π = 1.1 kHz),
below the unfiltered sideband error.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Photonic qubits encoded in discrete frequency bins
need low-loss, coherent devices that move light—and, in
quantum networks, single photons—between bins [1–3].
Single-photon frequency shifting is a motivating appli-
cation of this design, not a claim of a completed ex-
periment. TFLN combines strong Pockels, piezoelectric,
and photoelastic responses with low-loss resonators [4, 5],
and has been used for single-photon spectral shearing
over ±641 GHz [6], an integrated quantum frequency
processor [7], and coupled-cavity frequency beam split-
ters [8]. For single-tone shifting, the benchmark is the
EO coupled-ring shifter of Hu et al. [9]: a microwave
drive couples the symmetric (S) and antisymmetric (AS)
supermodes of a photonic molecule [10] and, at a gen-
eralized critical-coupling condition, transfers light uni-
directionally from one supermode to the other.
The
measured device shifts 98.7% of the output light by
28.2 GHz with 0.45 dB insertion loss (IL), and 99.1%
(up) and 99.4% (down) at 12.5 GHz, using 96 mW of mi-
crowave power [9]. A different EO approach, serrodyne
driving of cascaded traveling-wave TFLN phase mod-
ulators, reports a shifting efficiency above 98.8% with
Vπ ≈2.3 V [11].
Acoustic frequency shifting on TFLN is much less
mature.
The best published TFLN acousto-optic fre-
quency shifter, a traveling-wave Bragg-deflection device
at ∼3 GHz, reaches an on-chip efficiency of 3.5% at
30 dBm (1 W) applied to the IDT pads, with >30 dB
carrier suppression and a 70-MHz microwave band-
width [12].
Resonant acoustics improves the per-volt
modulation strongly: a suspended 100-µm acoustic res-
onator at 3.33 GHz gives Vπ = 4.6 V (VπL = 0.046 V cm)
on one arm of a Mach–Zehnder interferometer (MZI) [13].
Acoustic waves have long been used to modulate inte-
grated resonators [14] and to control photonic molecules
dynamically [15].
Most relevant here, Zhu et al. [16] have already driven
a lithium niobate (LN-on-sapphire) coupled-microring
photonic molecule acoustically. In their device an IDT-
launched 8.54-GHz acoustic wave forms a backward-
Brillouin dynamic Bragg mirror that couples forward-
and backward-propagating light, reaching strong super-
mode coupling at milliwatt-level acoustic power (cooper-
ativity 2.46 mW−1) and Bragg reflectivities up to 24%.
Acoustic driving of an LN photonic molecule is there-
fore not new.
The distinction of the present design is
its operating point: a forward (co-directional), single-
bus frequency shifter that sends ≈98% of the output into
one sideband, analyzed in terms of shift efficiency, in-
sertion loss, spurious orders, and the RF power at the
source. We also considered an all-optical, IDT-free alter-
native in which backward stimulated Brillouin scatter-
ing in TFLN, pumped by an EO frequency comb, writes
the phonon; in our (unpublished) analysis it is not vi-
able for multi-bin frequency operations, whereas forward-
Brillouin frequency-bin swaps have been proposed theo-
retically [17].[18]

brate its drive to measured data [Sec. II]; (ii) compute its
operating point and compare its source power with an EO

III.
OPERATING POINT AND DRIVE POWER
Ideal operating point (upper bound).—Sections III and
IV use nominal, idealized parameters (identical rings, no
fabrication error, no extra loss, perfect temperature con-
trol); the realistic-condition results that we regard as the
main prediction are given in Sec. V. At γ/2π = 0.6 GHz
the acoustic drive reaches its maximum ηshift = 98.3% at
δω/2π = 0.55 GHz, i.e. a source peak voltage of 26 mV
and PRF = 6.7 µW with the Shao IDT (Table I, Fig. 2).
The absolute efficiency is 55% and the IL is 2.5 dB; both
are set by the intrinsic loss rather than by the drive. The
shifted line is the q = +1 order. Relative to it, the carrier
is at −23.2 dB and the largest spurious order is q = −1
at −21.2 dB (q = +2 at −23.6 dB). These spurs come
from the common-mode phase modulation of single-ring
driving. The push-pull EO drive cancels this term and
reaches ηshift = 99.6%, with the carrier at −26.0 dB and
the q = −1 order at −30.6 dB [Fig. 2(b)], and the same
2.5 dB IL.
Comparison with EO driving.—Table II lists the source
power at the respective optima for each feed. Without
any matching on the EO side (Vc = V0) the EO drive
needs 3.1 mW, ≈460 times the 6.7 µW of the acoustic
drive, and the measured device of Ref. [9] used 96 mW.
These factors, however, compare a resonantly enhanced
acoustic drive with a non-resonant electrode, and they
do not survive a like-for-like comparison. With an LC
match the EO power falls to 13 µW (QL = 30), 4.8 µW
(QL = 100), or 1.2 µW (ideal inductor).
Against the
ideally matched IDT (3.4 µW), the two drives therefore
differ only by a factor of 0.36–3.9, and the EO drive is
the more power-efficient one when the inductor loss is
low. Against the as-measured Shao IDT (6.7 µW) the
range is 0.18–1.9. We therefore make no claim of a power
advantage over EO driving. The remaining differences
are qualitative: the acoustic resonator provides voltage
gain without an external matching network, but at the
cost of a ∼100-fold narrower RF bandwidth (Sec. IV) and
a fixed shift frequency.
Comparison with acousto-optic shifters.—The best
measured TFLN acousto-optic shifter reaches 3.5% on-
chip efficiency at 30 dBm (1 W) [12]. The present de-
sign reaches, in simulation, ηabs = 55% (ηshift = 98.3%)
at 6.7 µW under ideal conditions and a median on-chip
ηabs = 32.3% at 33.7 µW (Qm = 2000, 10% IDT effi-
ciency; heater-on κi × 2 Monte Carlo) under the realistic
conditions of Sec. V. The comparison is not like for like:
Ref. [12] is a broadband, non-resonant traveling-wave de-
flector with a 70-MHz microwave bandwidth, whereas our
design is a simulated, doubly resonant (optical and acous-
tic) device with a ∼1-MHz RF bandwidth and a single
fixed shift frequency; a measured device will also face
the fabrication and locking requirements of Sec. V. Zhu
et al. [16] operate an acoustic photonic molecule at mil-
liwatt acoustic power in a backward, reflective geometry
and do not report a forward shift efficiency or insertion
loss. Table III summarizes these benchmarks.

IV.
TOLERANCES
Optical quality factor.—Because the coupling γ is fixed
while κi varies, the shift ratio is robust but the loss is not
[Fig. 3(a)]. For Qi ≳1.3 × 106 we find ηshift ≈98%; at
Qi = 6.4 × 105 it drops to 82%. The absolute efficiency
rises from 33% at Qi = 1.3×106 through 55% at 2.4×106
to 87% at 9.7 × 106 (IL 0.56 dB). The required source
power stays between 6.1 and 8.5 µW over this range.
RF detuning.—The acoustic resonance filters the drive.
With Qm = 2000 (FWHM 1.67 MHz) we find ηshift ≥
96.7% within ±0.4 MHz of the doublet splitting, 80% at
1 MHz, 44% at 2 MHz, and 10% at 5 MHz [Fig. 3(b)].
The usable RF bandwidth is thus ∼1 MHz. Under the
flat-drive assumption, the LC-matched EO drive keeps
ηshift = 99.6% over the full ±5 MHz range.
IDT efficiency.—The acoustic source power scales as
1/ηIDT [Fig. 3(c)]: 3.4 µW at 100%, 6.7 µW at the Shao
IDT’s ≈50%, and 34 µW at 10%, a value reported for
other LN acousto-optic microrings [20].
The acoustic
drive needs more power than the QL = 30 LC-matched
EO drive (13 µW) once ηIDT ≲25%, more than the
QL = 100 match (4.8 µW) below ≈70%, and more
than the ideal-inductor match (1.2 µW) for any physi-
cal ηIDT ≤100%.
Thermal drift and ring mismatch.—We extend Eq. (1)
with a common laser–ring detuning ∆, a resonance mis-
match δr of ring 2 relative to ring 1, and a split-
ting error 2µ −Ωm, keeping the drive at the operating
point (Fig. 4); at zero offset we recover ηshift = 98.3%.
We find ηshift ≥95% for −72 ≤∆/2π ≤+55 MHz
(≥90% for −97 to +85 MHz), for −104 ≤δr/2π ≤
+145 MHz, and for −96 ≤(2µ −Ωm)/2π ≤+53 MHz.
Within the ≥95% region the IL stays between 2.50 and
2.66 dB. To convert to temperature we use |df/dT| ≈
f (dne/dT)/ng ≈2.8 GHz/K, with f = 193.4 THz and
dne/dT = 3.34 × 10−5 K−1 at 1523 nm [21].
With
the thermo-optic effect alone, a common drift must be
held within ≈±20 mK (±30 mK for ηshift ≥90%)
and the differential ring temperature within ±37 mK.
Thermal expansion, αa ≈1.54 × 10−5 K−1 [22], shifts
the resonances in the same direction; with |df/dT| ≈
f (dne/dT + neffαa)/ng ≈5.6 GHz/K (neff = 2.14 as-
sumed), consistent with the 5.6 GHz/K predicted for e-
polarized modes of a bulk LN resonator [23], the windows
shrink to ±9.8 mK (±15 mK for ηshift ≥90%) for com-
mon drift and ±18.6 mK for the differential temperature.

Heater on (main prediction).—Under IL column B and
10% IDT efficiency, the median ηshift = 96.4% (10th–90th

gives median IL 2.48 dB and on-chip absolute efficiency

power is 43 µW; the ξ sweep at the same IDT and Qm
gives 478/172/43/11 µW for ξ = 0.3/0.5/1/2 (Fig. 6).

VIII.
LIMITATIONS
The results above are design predictions subject to the
following limitations.
(i) No power advantage over matched EO. With
impedance matching on both sides the acoustic and EO
source powers differ only by a factor of 0.36–3.9, and the
LC-matched EO drive is more power-efficient with a low-
loss inductor (Sec. III). The advantage claimed here is
restricted to the class of acousto-optic frequency shifters.
(ii) Narrow RF bandwidth and fixed shift frequency.
The usable RF bandwidth is ∼1 MHz, set by the acous-
tic Qm = 2000, versus ≈122 MHz for a QL = 30 LC-
matched EO electrode and >3 GHz for the unmatched
device of Ref. [9].
The shift is fixed at the 3.33-GHz
acoustic resonance, whereas EO photonic molecules have
been operated at 12.5–28.2 GHz [9]. The RF source must
be frequency-stabilized, and the doublet splitting must
be thermally tuned to match Ωm to well within the op-
tical linewidth. Maintaining ηshift ≥95% requires active
stabilization of the common resonance at the ±9.8 mK
level when thermal expansion is included (±20 mK for
the thermo-optic effect alone; Sec. IV), e.g. with heater
electrodes or by locking the laser to the resonance; pas-
sive temperature control is insufficient.
The ring–ring
mismatch, in contrast, can be corrected with a heater
and is not a fundamental limit.
(iii) Insertion loss. The ideal IL is 2.5 dB (ηabs = 55%),
limited by the measured Qi ≈2.4×106 of the suspended
platform [13]; under realistic heater-on κi × 2 conditions
the median is 4.72 dB, compared with 0.45 dB for the
measured EO device [9]. The fiber-to-fiber estimate as-
sumes 1.5 dB per facet, which requires an optimized edge
coupler [24]; the suspended chip of Ref. [13] had ≲5 dB
per facet.
(iv) IDT efficiency. The acoustic drive loses to a QL =
30 LC-matched EO drive if the IDT efficiency is below
≈25%; IDT efficiencies of ≈10% have been reported in
related LN microring devices [20].
(v) Drive extrapolation and ξ. We obtain δω by extrap-
olating the measured MZI VπL of Ref. [13] linearly to a
ring that the acoustic field covers only partially (15%),
encoded as ξ = 1. Smaller ξ raises the required source
power as 1/ξ2; step 3 of Table V extracts ξ from RF
voltage versus optical sidebands. We assume the same
IDT efficiency, mode overlap, and uniform acoustic phase
along the covered section as in the MZI; ng = 2.3 is as-
sumed.
(vi) Heater residual and lock jitter (Sec. V). With the
MZI coupler heater on and µgap = µ
√

predicts that |2µ −Ωm| falls inside the ±1 MHz RF

a suspended device with an IDT, so the realistic IL is
likely pessimistic. The bulk thermal-expansion coefficient
likely overestimates the expansion of a bonded film.
(viii) Not modeled. We did not model ohmic and acous-
tic heating of the IDT and resonator, the resulting drive-
dependent drift of the ring resonances beyond the lock jit-
ter above (Ref. [13] observed a heating-induced red shift
with increasing microwave power), or the mechanical sta-
bility of the suspended structure.
(ix) Model provenance. Our implementation of Eq. (1)
is reconstructed from the published equations of Ref. [9],
not from the authors’ code, and the design combines pa-
rameters of two different devices (optics and electrodes
from Ref. [9], acoustics and loss from Ref. [13]).
Any
extension that routes the acoustic drive through on-chip
phononic circuits (for example, to feed several shifters)
would at present rely on acoustic-circuit parameters bor-
rowed from GaN-on-sapphire [25] and on our reconstruc-
tion of that model from its equations; none of the num-
bers reported here depend on it.
(x) Simulation only. No device has been fabricated;
every number in this paper, including the realistic Monte
Carlo, is a model prediction.
IX.
CONCLUSION
We have given a fabrication-oriented design for a
suspended-IDT acoustic resonator driving one ring of a
TFLN photonic molecule as a forward, single-bus fre-
quency shifter at 3.33 GHz, together with tolerances and
a five-step measurement plan. All numbers are predic-
tions. Relative to an EO drive of the same optics, the
Shao IDT needs ≈6.7 µW versus 1.2–13 µW when the
EO feed is matched: there is no claimed AO power ad-
vantage, and the AO bandwidth is ∼1 MHz. Relative
to Shao 2020, we compare absolute efficiency only (sim-
ulation, ∼1 MHz versus measured 3.5% at 1 W with
70 MHz). With the heater-on self-labeled MZI coupler,
conservative loss, and 10% IDT, the Monte Carlo median
ηshift is 96.4% (p10 91.7%); heater-off 94.5% is a compar-
ison only. IL is quoted both with Shao 2019 Qi (median
2.48 dB) and with assumed extra κi×2 (median 4.72 dB).
Conservative RF power at ξ = 0.5, Qm = 1570, and 10%
IDT is ≈172 µW. Unfiltered stage-1 spectroscopy should
track 1 −V ≈1 −ηshift; filtered HOM (1 −V = 8 × 10−7
at 10 MHz) is stage 2.
Thermal phonons contribute
nth/Ncoh ≈1.07 × 10−8 and nthC0 ≈8.0 × 10−6. Unlike
the backward-Brillouin photonic molecule of Ref. [16],
the present design is a forward, single-bus shifter and
reports ηshift explicitly. The five measurement steps of
Table V—heater tuning of 2µ, IDT S11/Qm, extrac-
tion of ξ, ηshift/IL/spurs, then narrowband HOM—are
the path from this prediction to data.
Under the as-
sumed coupler-isolation crosstalk, an internal tape-out
check on the conservative-loss Monte Carlo passes all
three gates (yield 94.0% at ηshift ≥90%; ≈172 µW; spur
q = −1): keep the AO device concept and proceed to
layout with those measurement hooks, but calibrate the
heater crosstalk before treating the yield gate as vali-
dated.
